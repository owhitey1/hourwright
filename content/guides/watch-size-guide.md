title: Watch size guide
description: How to read case diameter, lug-to-lug and thickness, and how to work out what will fit your wrist before you order online.
order: 4
---
Buying a watch online means judging fit from numbers. Three measurements tell you most of what you need.

## Measure your wrist first

Wrap a strip of paper or a soft tape around your wrist just above the wrist bone, mark where it meets, and measure the length in millimetres. Then measure the flat width across the top of your wrist. That width is the figure that limits how large a watch can be.

## Case diameter

This is the headline figure in every listing: the width of the case, excluding the crown. It tells you how much presence the watch has. It does not tell you whether it fits.

A watch with a wide bezel and small dial looks smaller than its diameter suggests. One that is nearly all dial looks larger.

## Lug-to-lug

This is the distance from the tip of the top lugs to the tip of the bottom lugs, and it is the measurement that decides fit. If the lugs overhang the flat of your wrist, the watch is too big for you, whatever the diameter says.

Not every retailer lists lug-to-lug. Where it is missing, the brand's own site or the watch's manual often has it.

## Thickness

Thickness decides whether the watch slides under a shirt cuff. Automatic chronographs and dive watches are the thickest. Hand-wound and quartz dress watches are the thinnest.

## Shapes that break the rules

- **Square and rectangular cases** wear larger than round ones of the same width.
- **Cushion cases** sit broad on the wrist.
- **Integrated bracelets** extend the visual length of the watch, because the first links do not articulate much.

## Bracelet sizing

Metal bracelets are supplied long and shortened by removing links. An authorised retailer will usually do this for you. Keep the spare links with the box: they matter if you ever sell the watch. Do not have a bracelet sized until you are sure you are keeping the watch, as an adjusted watch may not be returnable.
