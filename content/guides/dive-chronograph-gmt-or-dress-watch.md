title: Dive, chronograph, GMT or dress: which type of watch suits you

---
Most luxury watches fall into one of four families. Each was designed around a job, and that job still shapes how the watch looks, wears and what it costs to maintain.

## Dive watch

Built to time a dive and survive it. Expect a rotating bezel with a minute scale, luminous markers, a screw-down crown and water resistance of 200m or more.

Wears: sporty and substantial. The most versatile type for everyday use, because it is built to be knocked about.

Consider it if you want one watch for everything, including swimming. Check the thickness, as dive watches are the tallest of the four.

## Chronograph

A watch with a built-in stopwatch, operated by pushers on the side of the case, with small subdials recording elapsed time.

Wears: busy and technical. The dial carries more information, which many buyers love and some find cluttered.

Consider it if you like mechanical complexity. Bear in mind that a chronograph movement has more parts and costs more to service than a time-only watch.

## GMT

Shows a second time zone, using an extra hand that circles the dial once every 24 hours and reads against a 24-hour scale.

Wears: like a sports watch with one extra hand. The most practical complication if you travel or work with people abroad.

Consider it if you would genuinely use the second time zone. There are two kinds: on some the local hour hand jumps independently, which suits travellers, and on others the 24-hour hand is the adjustable one, which suits people tracking a distant office from home. Ask which you are buying.

## Dress watch

Slim, simple and usually on a leather strap. Hours and minutes, perhaps seconds and a date, and little else.

Wears: discreet and light, and slides under a shirt cuff.

Consider it if the watch is for work and formal occasions. Water resistance is usually modest, so it is not the one for the beach.

## Choosing between them

Three questions settle most decisions:

1. **Where will you wear it most?** Office and evenings point to a dress watch. Everything else points to a sports model.
2. **Will it go in water?** If yes, look for 100m or more and a bracelet or rubber strap.
3. **Do you want a function or a clean dial?** Chronographs and GMTs add capability and visual complexity in equal measure.

If you are buying one good watch to wear every day, a dive watch or a simple time-and-date sports watch on a bracelet is the safest choice. If you are adding a second, pick the type your first is not.
