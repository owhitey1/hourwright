A watch is a generous gift and an easy one to get slightly wrong. These steps reduce the risk.

## Find out what they already wear

Look at the watch they wear now. Note three things: whether it is on a metal bracelet or a strap, whether the case is steel or gold coloured, and roughly how large it sits on the wrist. People tend to stay close to what they know, so matching those three gets you most of the way.

## Choose a size that is hard to get wrong

If you cannot measure their wrist, stay in the middle of a model's size range. Very large and very small cases are personal choices. A bracelet can be shortened by removing links; a leather strap has holes and fits a range of wrists as supplied.

## Decide between quartz and mechanical

A mechanical watch needs wearing or winding and loses or gains a few seconds a day. Enthusiasts value that. Someone who simply wants a beautiful watch that is always right may prefer quartz. If you are unsure which camp they are in, our guide to movement types explains the difference.

## Protect the return

- **Check the retailer's returns policy before ordering**, including the period, the condition required and any extended seasonal returns.
- **Do not have the bracelet sized** or remove protective stickers until the recipient has seen it.
- **Hold off on engraving.** Personalised items are normally excluded from the right to cancel an online order. Engraving can be added later.
- **Keep the box, card and paperwork together.** An exchange will need all of them.

## Timing

Order earlier than you think you need to. Popular models go out of stock before major gifting dates, and delivery of high-value items usually needs a signature.

## The warranty card

The manufacturer's warranty starts on the date of sale, so it will be dated when you buy and not when you give it. For most gifts that is a matter of weeks and makes no practical difference. Make sure the card is stamped or activated and goes in the box.
