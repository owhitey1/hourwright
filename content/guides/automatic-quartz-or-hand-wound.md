The movement is the mechanism that keeps time. It is the largest single influence on what a watch costs and on what it is like to own.

## Quartz

A quartz watch is powered by a battery and regulated by a vibrating quartz crystal. It is more accurate than any mechanical watch, needs little attention beyond a battery change every few years, and can be left in a drawer and picked up running. Quartz versions of a luxury model usually cost noticeably less than the mechanical version.

Choose quartz if you want to set it and forget it, or if the watch will be worn only occasionally.

## Automatic

An automatic watch is mechanical. A weighted rotor swings as your wrist moves and winds the mainspring. Worn daily it keeps running; left alone it will stop when its power reserve runs out, typically after two to three days, though some modern movements last far longer.

Automatics gain or lose a few seconds a day and need periodic servicing by a watchmaker. In exchange you get the craft that most buyers at this level are paying for, often visible through a glass caseback.

Choose automatic if the watch will be worn most days and the mechanism matters to you.

## Hand-wound

A hand-wound, or manual, watch is mechanical without the rotor. You wind the crown yourself, usually once a day. With no rotor the movement can be thinner and the view through the caseback is unobstructed.

Choose hand-wound if you enjoy the ritual, or want the slimmest possible mechanical watch.

## Terms you will see in listings

- **Power reserve.** How long a fully wound mechanical watch runs before stopping.
- **In-house or manufacture calibre.** A movement designed and made by the brand itself, as opposed to one bought from a specialist supplier. In-house movements usually carry a price premium.
- **Chronometer.** A watch whose movement has passed an independent accuracy test. See our guide to certifications.
- **Complication.** Any function beyond hours, minutes and seconds: a date, a chronograph, a second time zone, a moonphase.

## Which costs more to own?

Mechanical watches cost more to maintain because servicing involves dismantling, cleaning and regulating the movement. Each brand publishes its own recommended service interval, and complicated movements such as chronographs cost more to service than simple ones. Ask the retailer what the brand recommends before you buy.
