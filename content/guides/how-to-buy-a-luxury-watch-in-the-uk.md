A luxury watch is one of the few purchases where the seller matters almost as much as the product. This guide covers the checks that protect you, in the order you will meet them.

## Buy from an authorised retailer

An authorised retailer is appointed by the brand to sell its watches. Buying from one means the watch is new, genuine and covered by the manufacturer's international warranty from the date of sale. Every retailer we link to states that it is authorised for the brands it sells, and you can confirm this on the brand's own store locator.

Watches sold outside that network are often called grey market. They can be genuine and cheaper, but the manufacturer's warranty may not apply. We do not list grey-market sellers.

## Check exactly which watch is being quoted

Two watches that look alike can differ in ways that move the price considerably. Before comparing prices, match these details:

- **Reference number.** This is the manufacturer's code for one precise configuration. If the references match, the watches match.
- **Case size and metal.** Many models come in several diameters and in steel, two-tone or gold.
- **Movement.** Some lines are sold in both quartz and automatic versions.
- **Strap or bracelet.** A metal bracelet usually costs more than leather or rubber, and is expensive to add later.

## Understand the warranty

Manufacturer warranties on new watches run for a set number of years that varies by brand, and some brands extend cover if you register the watch. The retailer's product page should state the period. Keep the stamped or activated warranty card: you will need it for any claim and it supports the value of the watch if you sell.

## Know your return rights

When you buy online from a UK retailer you normally have a legal right to cancel within 14 days of delivery under the Consumer Contracts Regulations. Exceptions include personalised items, so an engraved watch may not be returnable. Retailers may also require the watch to be unworn with protective stickers intact. Read the retailer's returns page before you order, and do not size the bracelet until you are sure.

## Paying for it

Many retailers offer finance. We are not a credit broker and nothing on this site is financial advice. If you are considering credit, read the retailer's terms carefully and check the total amount repayable.

## On delivery

Inspect the watch before wearing it. Check the reference and serial numbers against the paperwork, confirm the warranty card is dated, and tell your home insurer. High-value watches often need to be listed individually on a policy to be covered away from home.
