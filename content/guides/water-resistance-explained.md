The depth printed on a dial is a laboratory pressure rating. It is not the depth you can take the watch to. This is the most misunderstood figure in watch buying.

## What the ratings mean in practice

- **30m (3 bar).** Splash resistant. Rain and hand washing only.
- **50m (5 bar).** Brief immersion. Most makers advise against swimming.
- **100m (10 bar).** Swimming and snorkelling.
- **200m to 300m (20 to 30 bar).** Recreational scuba diving, particularly where the watch is a certified dive watch.

Manufacturers set their own guidance, so check the manual for your model. The ratings assume a new watch with intact seals.

## What makes a true dive watch

The international standard for dive watches is ISO 6425. It requires a minimum rating of 100m, a way of timing a dive such as a unidirectional rotating bezel, legibility in the dark and additional testing of each watch. A watch marked "Diver's" followed by a depth is claiming compliance with that standard.

## The parts that keep water out

- **Screw-down crown.** Threads into the case and compresses a gasket. It must be screwed fully home before the watch goes near water.
- **Gaskets.** Rubber or synthetic seals at the crown, caseback and crystal. They harden with age.
- **Chronograph pushers.** Unless the manual says otherwise, do not press them under water.

## Looking after the seals

Water resistance is not permanent. Have the watch pressure tested periodically and whenever the caseback has been opened, for example for a battery change. Rinse it in fresh water after sea or pool use. Avoid hot showers and saunas, because heat and steam are harder on seals than depth.

## Leather straps

A watch can be rated to 100m and still be unsuitable for swimming if it is on a leather strap. If you plan to swim in it, compare the bracelet or rubber strap version.
