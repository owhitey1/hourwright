A good watch can last for decades, but choosing one for someone else means guessing his size, his taste and how he will wear it. These five cover different kinds of man rather than ranking one above another: a diver for every day, a pilot's chronograph, a design-led automatic, a travel watch and a chronometer from a newer independent brand. All were in stock and between about £2,000 and £3,200 when we published; the cards show today's retailer price.

If you are buying for a woman as well, see our companion guide, [luxury watches for her this Christmas](luxury-watches-for-her-christmas.html).

## Before you choose

**Size.** Look at a watch he already wears and likes. Many men's watches sit between 38 and 42mm, and 39 or 40mm suits many wrists, so it is a reasonable guess. Thickness matters too: a chronograph like the Hamilton is noticeably taller than a three-hand watch. Our [watch size guide](../guides/watch-size-guide.html) explains how diameter, thickness and lug-to-lug length change the fit.

**Style.** A dive watch on a steel bracelet works with most clothes and can be worn swimming. A more design-led watch says more about taste. If you are unsure, sporty and versatile is the lower-risk choice.

**Movement.** All five are mechanical. Four wind themselves as they are worn; the Hamilton is hand-wound, which some people enjoy as a daily ritual and others find a chore. An automatic with a short power reserve will stop if left off for a couple of days. Our guide to [automatic, quartz and hand-wound movements](../guides/automatic-quartz-or-hand-wound.html) explains the difference.

## For one watch that does everything: Oris Divers Date 39mm

The Divers Date is a modern dive watch at a sensible 39mm. Oris lists a black dial, a ceramic bezel insert, a sapphire crystal domed on both sides and 20 bar (200 metres) of water resistance, with the Oris 733-1 automatic movement and 41 hours of power reserve. This "Set" reference comes with a steel bracelet and an additional black rubber strap, which Oris says can be changed quickly, so he gets two looks in one box.

Consider: 41 hours means it may need resetting after a weekend off the wrist. Oris gives two years' warranty as standard and three years when the watch is registered with MyOris.

## For a pilot's chronograph with character: Hamilton Khaki Aviation Pioneer Mechanical Chrono

The Pioneer chronograph is a hand-wound pilot's watch in a 40mm steel case. Hamilton lists a blue sunray dial, a sapphire crystal, 100-metre water resistance and the H-51-Si manual-winding chronograph movement with 60 hours of power reserve. It comes on a steel bracelet with a folding clasp.

Consider: it is 14.35mm thick with a 50mm lug-to-lug length, so it is likely to wear larger than its diameter suggests. He will need to wind it by hand. Hamilton gives a two-year warranty from purchase.

## For a design-led everyday watch: Raymond Weil Freelancer Calibre RW1212

This Freelancer is cushion-shaped rather than round, measuring 40 by 40mm and 10.5mm thick. Raymond Weil lists a gradient green dial with an opening at 6 o'clock that shows the balance wheel, the self-winding RW1212 movement that the brand describes as in-house, 41 hours of power reserve (the retailer's description gives a different figure; we use the maker's), a sapphire caseback, a screw-down crown and 100-metre water resistance. It comes on a steel bracelet with a double push-button folding clasp.

Consider: a cushion case and green dial are a confident choice, so this suits someone whose taste you know well. Raymond Weil gives two years' warranty, plus a third year when the watch is registered with its WEIL Club.

## For the frequent traveller: Longines HydroConquest GMT 41mm

The HydroConquest GMT adds a second time zone to Longines' dive watch. Longines lists a 41mm steel case with a ceramic bezel, a sunray black dial, 300-metre water resistance and the L844 automatic movement with up to 72 hours of power reserve and a silicon balance spring, and describes it as resistant to magnetic fields. It is on a steel bracelet with a double-safety folding clasp.

Consider: the GMT function only earns its keep if he travels or keeps in touch across time zones, and at 41mm it is one of the larger watches here. Longines gives mechanical watches bought from an official dealer a five-year warranty. Our [HydroConquest vs Oris Aquis Date comparison](longines-hydroconquest-vs-oris-aquis-date.html) covers the wider HydroConquest range.

## For something less obvious: Norqain Adventure 40mm

The Adventure is Norqain's sports watch. Norqain lists a 40mm steel case 12.55mm thick with a 48.3mm lug-to-lug length, a grey dial with the brand's mountain pattern, a unidirectional bezel, a double-domed sapphire crystal, a sapphire caseback and 200-metre water resistance. The Norqain N08 automatic movement, which the brand states is based on the Sellita SW 200-1, is COSC-certified as a chronometer, with 41 hours of power reserve. It comes on an integrated steel bracelet.

Consider: Norqain is less widely known than the other names here, which some people like and others do not. Norqain's product page describes a two-year warranty plus one year with a MyNorqain account.

## Buying it as a gift

Buy from an authorised retailer so the manufacturer's warranty applies, and keep the warranty card with the receipt. Check the retailer's own Christmas delivery dates, bracelet-sizing options and returns window before ordering, especially if you are guessing his size.
