If you have about £2,000 to spend on a Swiss dive watch, the Longines HydroConquest and the Oris Aquis Date are two obvious candidates. Both are steel, both have ceramic on the bezel, both are rated to 300 metres, and both were around £2,000 to £2,200 at Goldsmiths when we published. The differences are in the size range, the movement and the warranty, and those are what should decide it.

## Size: the most important difference

The HydroConquest in its 2026 form comes in 39mm and 42mm, and Longines publishes the same 11.7mm thickness for both. The 48.1mm lug-to-lug length of the 39mm suits most wrists, including slimmer ones; the 42mm, at 51.2mm, is likely to wear noticeably larger.

The Aquis Date in our comparison is 36.5mm or 43.5mm, with a 41.5mm version also listed in our catalogue. The 43.5mm Aquis has a broad, integrated look and a 23mm lug width, so it is likely to feel substantial. The 36.5mm suits small wrists or anyone who wants a smaller dive watch.

If you are between sizes, our [watch size guide](../guides/watch-size-guide.html) explains why lug-to-lug length often matters more than diameter.

## Movement and power reserve

Longines names the L888.5 for the 2026 HydroConquest: an automatic with up to 72 hours of power reserve and a silicon balance spring. Longines states that the movement resists magnetic fields to the ISO 764 standard, and Swatch Group's announcement puts it at up to ten times that standard. In practice, 72 hours means you can take it off on Friday evening and it will still be running on Monday morning.

Oris lists Calibre 733-1 for the Aquis Date: an automatic at 4Hz with 41 hours of power reserve. That is a little under two days, so if it sits unworn over a weekend you will probably need to reset it. Oris shows the movement through a see-through caseback.

## Bracelet and clasp

The HydroConquest references here are on steel bracelets. The black-dial 39mm (L3.779.4.90.6) is on a Milanese mesh bracelet, which Goldsmiths' name for it, "Black Mesh", refers to. Longines lists the blue 42mm (L3.788.4.99.6) on a steel mesh bracelet too. Both use a double-folding safety clasp with push-piece opening and micro-adjustment. If you prefer a conventional link bracelet, check the dial and reference before you buy, because Longines offers several HydroConquest combinations.

Both Aquis Date versions are on steel bracelets; Oris lists a multi-piece bracelet with a security folding clasp for the 36.5mm. Oris does not describe a quick-adjust extension on the 733-1 models we checked, so look at the clasp details on the retailer's page if on-the-wrist adjustment matters to you.

## Warranty

This is a real difference. Longines gives mechanical watches bought from an official dealer (from 1 January 2021) a five-year warranty; straps and bracelets are covered for 24 months. Oris gives two years as standard and extends the Aquis Date to three years when you register the watch with MyOris, its free owner programme. Keep the warranty paperwork from the retailer in either case.

## Which suits you?

**Choose the HydroConquest if** you want the 39mm or 42mm size, the longer 72-hour power reserve, a silicon balance spring and the five-year warranty. The 39mm is the more versatile of the two sizes.

## The step-up models

Each brand offers a version with a more capable movement, for a few hundred pounds to a little over £1,000 more when we published.

**Longines HydroConquest GMT 41mm.** Longines' L844 calibre adds a second time zone, keeping the 72-hour power reserve and silicon balance spring, and Longines describes it as resistant to magnetic fields. The case is 41mm with a ceramic bezel, on a steel bracelet with a double-safety folding clasp, rated to 300 metres. It suits a diver who also travels.

{{product:goldsmiths-42293040246}}

**Oris Aquis Date Calibre 400.** Oris' in-house Calibre 400 has 120 hours of power reserve, five days. Oris states over 30 non-ferrous parts for magnetic resistance, accuracy of -3 to +5 seconds a day, a recommended 10-year service interval, and a 10-year warranty when the watch is registered with MyOris. This 43.5mm version has Oris' quick-adjust clasp, which lets you lengthen or shorten the bracelet without tools. It removes the two main reasons to hesitate over the standard Aquis Date: the short power reserve and the shorter warranty.
