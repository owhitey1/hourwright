Longines is the largest brand in our catalogue, with nearly 100 watches listed from £2,000 to £3,000 alone. That makes choosing harder, not easier. These five are chosen to cover different kinds of buyer rather than to rank one above another. Every price was under £3,000 when we published; the cards below show today's price.

## How they compare

| Watch | Type | Case | Movement | Water resistance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Master Collection 40mm | Dress and everyday | 40mm steel, bracelet | L888 automatic, 72 hours | Not stated by the retailer |
| Spirit Pilot 39mm | Pilot's watch | 39mm steel, rubber strap | L888.4 automatic, COSC chronometer, 72 hours | 100 metres |
| HydroConquest 39mm, Glasgow 2026 edition | Dive watch, external bezel | 39mm steel, ceramic bezel | Automatic, 72 hours | 300 metres |

## For one watch that does everything: Master Collection 40mm

The Master Collection, introduced in 2005, is Longines' classic line. This version pairs a 40mm steel case and bracelet with an olive green sunray dial, applied Roman numerals and a date at 3 o'clock. The L888 automatic movement has a 72-hour power reserve. It is the most formal of the five, and the bracelet makes it easy to wear daily.

Consider: the retailer does not state its water resistance, so treat it as a watch to keep out of the pool unless Longines confirms otherwise.

## For legibility and a cleaner dial: Spirit Pilot 39mm

Introduced as a simpler three-hand Spirit, the Spirit Pilot drops the date and the five stars of earlier Spirit models for a plain matt black dial with gold-coloured hands and numerals. Longines states that Spirit Pilot movements carry COSC chronometer certification and a silicon balance spring; the case has a screw-in crown and 100-metre water resistance.

Consider: there is no date, and this version is on a rubber strap with a buckle.

## For a dive watch with a story: HydroConquest 39mm, Glasgow 2026 edition

This is the limited edition Longines made for the Glasgow 2026 Commonwealth Games, of which it was official timekeeper. It has the full HydroConquest specification: 300 metres, a unidirectional ceramic bezel, screw-in crown, screw-down caseback and a 72-hour automatic movement with a silicon balance spring. The teal gradient dial follows the Games' colours, and production is limited to 2,026 pieces per size.

Consider: the commemorative colours and caseback engraving are the point here. If you want a plainer HydroConquest, our dive watch collection lists the standard versions.

{{product:goldsmiths-45505230021}}

## For a different take on the dive watch: Legend Diver 39mm

Our current [Watch of the Week](watch-of-the-week-longines-legend-diver-39mm.html). Its dive bezel sits inside the case and is turned by a second crown, a layout Longines first used in 1959. Specification matches the HydroConquest on depth and power reserve, with the L888.6 calibre.

Consider: the internal bezel is slower to set, and it costs more than the HydroConquest.

{{product:goldsmiths-41297899165}}

## For frequent travellers: Spirit Zulu Time 39mm

The Zulu Time adds a 24-hour hand and a time-zone mechanism, so you can read a second time zone at a glance, using the L844.4 calibre. This version has a blue ceramic bezel, a sunray blue dial, a date at 6 o'clock and a leather strap, with 100-metre water resistance.

Consider: it is the most expensive of the five and the closest to the £3,000 ceiling, and a leather strap is not ideal for water.

## Before you choose

All five use Longines' own automatic movements and stainless steel cases, so the decision is mainly about use: a bracelet dress watch, a clean pilot's watch, one of two dive watches, or a travel watch. Check the [size guide](../guides/watch-size-guide.html) against your wrist, since four of these are 39mm and one is 40mm, and compare the rest of the range in our [Longines brand guide](../brands/longines.html).
