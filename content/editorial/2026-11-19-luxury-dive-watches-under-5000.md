Most luxury dive watches will never go deeper than a hotel pool, but the features that make them work underwater, a readable dial, a timing bezel, a screw-down crown and real water resistance, also make them practical everyday watches. These six span four makers and about £2,000 to £3,500 when we published, all comfortably under £5,000. The cards show today's retailer price.

## What to check before you buy

**Water resistance and ISO 6425.** 100 metres is fine for swimming; 200 to 300 metres is the usual standard for a dive watch. ISO 6425 is the international standard for divers' watches: it covers watches built for diving to at least 100 metres, with a secure way of timing a dive that can be read in the dark, and it sets the tests a watch must pass before it can be marked as a diver's watch. None of the six makers' pages here mentions ISO 6425. Seiko's is the only one that describes its watch as a 300-metre diver's model; the others give a water-resistance rating without that label. Our guide to [water resistance](../guides/water-resistance-explained.html) explains what the ratings mean in practice.

**The bezel.** A unidirectional bezel only turns one way, so a knock can only shorten your timed dive, never lengthen it. Longines' Legend Diver puts the bezel inside the crystal instead, set by a second crown at 2 o'clock.

**Size.** The 39mm watches here suit a wide range of wrists. The Rado, Seiko and Aquis Calibre 400 are larger, and the Seiko is 13.4mm thick. Our [watch size guide](../guides/watch-size-guide.html) explains why lug-to-lug length matters.

**Bracelet or rubber.** A steel bracelet is harder-wearing and dressier, but it needs a clasp extension to fit over a wetsuit sleeve. Rubber is lighter, dries quickly and is the simpler choice for swimming. The Oris Divers Date set comes with both, the Legend Diver here is on rubber, the Seiko's clasp has an extender, and the rest are on steel bracelets or mesh.

**Power reserve, warranty and servicing.** These vary more than you might expect: from 41 hours to five days, and from two years' warranty to ten. Oris is the only maker here to publish a recommended service interval, 10 years for the Calibre 400; for the others, follow the guidance in the warranty booklet and have water resistance checked regularly if you swim with the watch.

## For a first dive watch at a sensible size: Oris Divers Date 39mm

Oris lists a 39mm steel case, a black dial, a ceramic bezel insert, a sapphire crystal domed on both sides and 200-metre water resistance, with the Oris 733-1 automatic movement and 41 hours of power reserve. This "Set" reference comes with both a steel bracelet and a black rubber strap.

Consider: it has the shortest power reserve here, and Oris' standard warranty is two years, extended to three when registered with MyOris.

## For a five-year warranty at a sensible size: Longines HydroConquest 39mm

The 2026 HydroConquest has a 39mm steel case, a unidirectional bezel with a ceramic insert, a sapphire crystal with anti-reflective coating on both sides and 300-metre water resistance. Longines names the L888.5 automatic movement with up to 72 hours of power reserve and a silicon balance spring. This black-dial reference is on a Milanese mesh bracelet. We compare it in detail in [Longines HydroConquest vs Oris Aquis Date](longines-hydroconquest-vs-oris-aquis-date.html).

Consider: the Milanese mesh bracelet is a distinctive look that will not suit everyone. Longines gives mechanical watches bought from an official dealer a five-year warranty.

## For something different: Longines Legend Diver 39mm

The Legend Diver keeps its timing bezel inside the case, under the crystal, turned by the screw-down crown at 2 o'clock. Longines lists a 39mm steel case, 300-metre water resistance and the L888.6 automatic movement with up to 72 hours of power reserve. This reference is on a rubber strap. We covered it as a [Watch of the Week](watch-of-the-week-longines-legend-diver-39mm.html).

Consider: an internal bezel is slower to set than an external one, and the vintage styling will not suit everyone.

## For a long power reserve: Rado Captain Cook Automatic 42mm

Rado lists a 42mm polished steel case 12.3mm thick, a blue and white dial with a date, a rotating bezel with a blue high-tech ceramic insert, a sapphire crystal, a transparent sapphire caseback and 300-metre water resistance. The R763 automatic movement has 80 hours of power reserve and an antimagnetic Nivachron hairspring. Rado delivers it on a steel bracelet and lists a blue leather strap and a blue-and-white textile strap as options; check with the retailer what comes in the box. Rado gives a five-year warranty.

Consider: Rado does not state that the bezel is unidirectional. At 42mm it is larger than the Oris and Longines models.

## For a Japanese professional diver: Seiko Prospex Marinemaster 1968 Heritage

Seiko describes this as a 300-metre diver’s watch. It has a 42.6mm steel case 13.4mm thick with a 49.3mm lug-to-lug length, a unidirectional rotating bezel, a dual-curved sapphire crystal, a screw-down crown and LumiBrite on the hands, indexes and bezel. The 8L35 automatic movement runs at 28,800 beats an hour with about 50 hours of power reserve, and Seiko states accuracy of +15 to -10 seconds a day. The three-fold clasp has a secure lock, a push-button release and an extender. Seiko gives a three-year warranty.

Consider: at 13.4mm it is a thick watch, and Seiko's accuracy tolerance is wider than a chronometer's.

## For the longest power reserve and warranty: Oris Aquis Date Calibre 400 43.5mm

Oris' in-house Calibre 400 gives five days (120 hours) of power reserve. Oris states over 30 non-ferrous parts for magnetic resistance, accuracy of -3 to +5 seconds a day, a recommended 10-year service interval, and a 10-year warranty when registered with MyOris. The 43.5mm steel case has a ceramic top ring, a sapphire crystal and 300-metre water resistance, on a steel bracelet with a security folding clasp with extension.

Consider: it is the largest and most expensive watch here.
